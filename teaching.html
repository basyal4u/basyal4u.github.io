Learning philosophy

Understand → Experiment → Explain → Reflect

Students first develop a mental model, then manipulate an interactive system, explain what changed and why, and finally consider responsible use and limitations.

Course lab pattern

Every lab is assignment-ready.

Each module now includes a learning guide, multi-variable experiment, required run sequence, evidence notebook, 100-point rubric, report-format example, and personalized checkpoint.

1

Learn the mechanism

Students connect intuition to the underlying model, equation, or algorithm before changing settings.

2

Run controlled experiments

Students change their own variables, sentences, data, thresholds, or sampling controls and record multiple comparisons.

3

Capture evidence

A session code, run IDs, screenshots, and downloadable evidence CSV tie the written analysis to work completed in the lab.

4

Explain in their own words

Reports are student-authored and graded on evidence, technical reasoning, interpretation, limitations, and reflection—not on getting one “perfect” output.

Why this format

AI becomes clearer when students can manipulate it.

The lab is intentionally browser-first: no software installation and no paid model API are required for the foundational exercises. The coursework design also emphasizes process evidence and student authorship rather than automated AI-writing detector scores.

Low technical barrier

Browser-first experiments help students focus on concepts before tooling complexity.

Visible cause and effect

Sliders and visual outputs make parameters, thresholds, and model behavior tangible.

Responsible interpretation

Activities distinguish educational simulations from production AI and emphasize limitations.
