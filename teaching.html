Evidence-informed course design

A teaching framework built for durable learning.

Technology serves the pedagogy—not the reverse. Every tool must make disciplinary thinking more visible, feedback more useful, or authentic practice more accessible.

01

Constructive alignment

Outcomes specify what students should be able to do; learning activities rehearse that performance; assessments ask students to demonstrate it with evidence.

Biggs, Higher Education ↗
02

Active, generative learning

Students predict, manipulate, compare, explain, and reflect. The labs move beyond passive exposure by making the learner construct and defend an interpretation.

Freeman et al., PNAS ↗
03

Formative feedback

Low-stakes checks surface missing evidence before final submission. Feedback is tied to transparent rubric criteria and tells students what action to take next.

Black & Wiliam ↗
04

Inclusive learner agency

Browser-first access, captions, voice guidance, examples, multiple representations, and alternative participation routes reduce unnecessary barriers without reducing rigor.

CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0 ↗
The student learning cycle

Understand → Experiment → Evidence → Explain → Transfer

Each stage produces an observable learning behavior rather than a vague promise of engagement.

1

Understand

Build a mental model using precise concepts, visual explanations, and the mathematics underneath.

2

Experiment

Change one condition at a time and observe cause, effect, tradeoffs, and failure modes.

3

Evidence

Capture settings, outputs, calculations, screenshots, and reproducible run records.

4

Explain

Use disciplinary language to explain why the observed behavior is plausible and limited.

5

Transfer

Turn analysis into a responsible recommendation for a new business or societal context.

Quality made visible

What strong course design looks like in practice.

Teaching quality is communicated through the decisions embedded in the course: intellectual coherence, current disciplinary content, authentic work, equitable access, timely feedback, and evidence of student reasoning.

AlignedOutcomes, activities, assessment, and rubrics point to the same capabilities.
CurrentAI, LLM, agentic, machine-vision, Excel, and R content connects foundations with contemporary practice.
AuthenticStudents solve individualized business problems rather than reproduce one answer.
TransparentExpectations, evidence standards, limitations, and authorship policies are explicit.
ReflectiveStudents explain uncertainty, ethics, failure modes, and what they would test next.
ReproducibleRun logs, formulas, code, workbooks, and evidence exports connect claims to completed work.
