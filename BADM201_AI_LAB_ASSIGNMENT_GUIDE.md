# BADM-201 Interactive AI Lab — Assignment & Student Deliverable Guide

## Purpose
The AI Lab is an experimentation environment, not a worksheet with one correct output. Students change inputs, model settings, and assumptions; record multiple runs; compare results; connect observations to the underlying mathematics; and write their own interpretation.

## Standard submission package for every lab
1. Student-authored report (recommended 2–4 pages plus evidence appendix).
2. Required number of distinct experimental runs shown in the Assignment tab.
3. Results table that lists the settings changed and outputs observed.
4. At least two screenshots supporting claims made in the report.
5. Exported AI Lab Evidence CSV from the Experiment Notebook.
6. Session code on the first page of the report.
7. Response to the personalized checkpoint shown in that lab.
8. Signed/typed authorship statement.

## Student-authorship / generative-AI policy
Unless the instructor explicitly changes the policy for a specific assignment, generative AI tools may not draft, rewrite, paraphrase, summarize, or generate the report text, interpretations, reflection, answers, or personalized checkpoint response.

Permitted by default: ordinary spelling/grammar highlighting that does not generate replacement prose, accessibility tools, and instructor-approved accommodations.

Verification is evidence-based rather than detector-based. The instructor may compare the report with the student's recorded run evidence/screenshots and may ask the student to explain one result orally. Automated AI-writing detector scores are not treated as proof of authorship.

## Standard 100-point rubric
- Experimental design & completion — 20
- Evidence & results — 20
- Technical explanation — 20
- Interpretation & comparison — 20
- Critical reflection — 10
- Communication & authorship — 10

## Lab 1 — Neural Network Playground
Minimum runs: 5. Compare dataset, learning rate, hidden-unit count, activation function, and noise. Explain decision-boundary behavior using forward propagation, nonlinear activation, loss, and gradient updates.

## Lab 2 — k-Nearest Neighbors
Minimum runs: 5. Compare k, distance metric, query location, and dataset geometry. Explain local voting and why neighborhood choice changes predictions.

## Lab 3 — What-If Explainable AI
Minimum runs: 5. Compare feature values, threshold, and scenarios. Distinguish model score, contribution, decision threshold, explanation, causality, and fairness.

## Lab 4 — Sentiment & Language Failure Modes
Minimum runs: 6. Create original positive, negative, mixed, negated, sarcastic, and domain-specific sentences. Compare baseline and negation-aware rules and diagnose failure cases.

## Lab 5 — Gradient Descent
Minimum runs: 5. Compare learning rates, momentum, start points, and loss surfaces. Explain convergence, instability, and local behavior using the gradient update.

## Lab 6 — K-Means Clustering
Minimum runs: 5. Compare k, initialization, and dataset shape. Interpret inertia and discuss why mathematical clusters are not automatically meaningful real-world groups.

## Lab 7 — AI or Human? Calibration Challenge
Minimum attempts: 6. Record confidence and evidence cues before each decision. Evaluate accuracy and calibration; explain why stylistic cues are insufficient proof of AI authorship.

## Lab 8 — LLM Sampling Explorer
Minimum runs: 6. Compare temperature, top-k, top-p, and custom logits. Use entropy and repeated sampling to explain determinism versus diversity.

## Strong report structure
1. Purpose
2. Experimental design
3. Results table
4. Interpretation tied to specific runs
5. Connection to the relevant mathematics/concepts
6. Limitation / critical reflection
7. Personalized checkpoint response
8. Evidence appendix
9. Authorship statement
