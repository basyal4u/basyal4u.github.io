Lab 1 · Deep learning foundations

Neural Network Playground

2 inputs → 2 hidden neurons → 1 output

Why this matters

A neural network learns useful internal representations by changing weights and biases to reduce error. The same idea scales from this tiny network to much larger systems used for images, language, forecasting, and decision support.

How to play

  1. Start with the untrained network and inspect its decision surface.
  2. Click Train 1 epoch and watch the loss and accuracy change.
  3. Click Train 50 epochs several times. Notice how the nonlinear decision surface adapts.
  4. Change the learning rate. Very small values learn slowly; overly large values can become unstable.
  5. Hover over the colored grid to inspect the predicted probability at any coordinate.
  6. Hover over h₁, h₂, or ŷ to see what each neuron does.
Math underneath

Forward pass

z = w₁x₁ + w₂x₂ + b
σ(z) = 1 / (1 + e−z)
ŷ = σ(v₁h₁ + v₂h₂ + bo)

The hidden neurons apply nonlinear transformations. The output neuron converts the final score into a probability between 0 and 1.

Learning

w ← w − η ∂L/∂w

η is the learning rate. L is binary cross-entropy loss. Backpropagation computes how each weight contributed to the error.

Lab 2 · Supervised learning

k-Nearest Neighbors Classification

Why this matters

k-NN makes a prediction from nearby labeled examples. It is simple, interpretable, and useful for understanding distance-based learning.

How to play

  1. Move the unknown point with the X and Y sliders.
  2. Change k, the number of neighbors that vote.
  3. Hover over training points to inspect their class and coordinates.
  4. Compare small versus large k and observe the stability of the prediction.

Math underneath

d(p,q)=√[(p₁−q₁)²+(p₂−q₂)²]
ŷ = mode(y of k nearest points)

k-NN has almost no training phase; most computation occurs when a new point is classified.

Prediction—
Think about it: Why can k=1 overreact to noise? Why can a very large k erase useful local structure?
Lab 3 · Explainability

Explainable AI Lab

Why this matters

Prediction alone is often not enough. In high-stakes contexts, people may need to understand which features pushed a decision upward or downward.

How to play

  1. Move each feature slider.
  2. Watch the score and contribution bars change.
  3. Identify the strongest positive and negative drivers.
  4. Ask whether an explanation also proves that a model is fair or appropriate.

Math underneath

z = b + Σ wᵢxᵢ
score = σ(z)

This educational model exposes each weighted contribution directly. Real explainability methods such as SHAP approximate similar contribution ideas for much more complex models.

Synthetic score—
Reflection: A transparent explanation can help inspect a decision, but does transparency alone guarantee fairness, causal validity, or ethical use?

How to play

Lab 5 · Optimization

Gradient Descent Visualizer

Why this matters

Most neural networks are trained by repeatedly moving parameters in a direction that reduces loss.

How to play

  1. Choose a learning rate.
  2. Press Start.
  3. Watch the parameter move downhill.
  4. Reset and compare different learning rates.

Math underneath

θt+1 = θt − η∇L(θt)

The gradient tells us the local direction of steepest increase; subtracting it moves downhill.

Current state—
Challenge: Find a learning rate that converges quickly and one that behaves poorly. Explain why.
Lab 6 · Unsupervised learning

K-Means Clustering Playground

How to play

Change k between 2 and 5 and observe how the same unlabeled points are partitioned. Generate a fresh dataset and see whether the same k remains sensible.

Math underneath

arg min Σ ||xᵢ − μc(i)||²

K-means alternates between assigning points to the nearest centroid and moving each centroid to the mean of its assigned points.

Result—
Think about it: Does every mathematical cluster correspond to a meaningful real-world group?

Lab 8 · Generative AI

LLM Temperature Explorer

How to play

Move temperature from low to high and watch the next-token distribution flatten. Lower values concentrate probability on the most likely choice; higher values increase diversity.

Math underneath

P(i) = exp(zᵢ/T) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ/T)

Dividing logits by temperature T changes the sharpness of the softmax distribution before sampling.

Important: this is a transparent probability simulation, not a live language model.
