Lab 1 · Deep learning foundations

Neural Network Playground

classification + backpropagation

Why this matters

Neural networks learn nonlinear decision rules by combining many simple weighted computations. The same training principles used here—forward pass, loss, gradient, backpropagation, and parameter updates—scale to image recognition, forecasting, language models, and other deep-learning systems.

What to look for

  • Decision boundary: how the model partitions the feature space.
  • Capacity: more hidden units can represent more complicated boundaries.
  • Activation: nonlinear activations make curved or piecewise boundaries possible.
  • Learning rate: controls step size during optimization.
  • Noise: makes the learning problem less clean and can reveal overfitting.

hⱼ = activation(zⱼ)
ŷ = σ(Σⱼ vⱼhⱼ + bₒ)
L = −[y log ŷ + (1−y)log(1−ŷ)]
w ← w − η ∂L/∂w

Lab 2 · Supervised learning

k-Nearest Neighbors Classification

distance + local voting

Why this matters

k-NN is an intuitive example of instance-based learning. It shows why scaling, distance choice, neighborhood size, noisy observations, and local density can change a prediction.

What to investigate

Compare small and large k, Euclidean versus Manhattan distance, and clean versus overlapping datasets. Move the query point near a class boundary and watch how the vote changes.

Math underneath

d₂(p,q)=√Σᵢ(pᵢ−qᵢ)²
d₁(p,q)=Σᵢ|pᵢ−qᵢ|
ŷ = mode(labels of k nearest neighbors)

What-If Explainable AI

Why this matters

Explanations can help users inspect a model, identify dominant factors, and conduct what-if analysis. But an explanation is not the same as causality, fairness, or proof that a model should be deployed.

This lab uses a deliberately transparent synthetic score so the contribution arithmetic is visible.

Lab 6 · Unsupervised learning

K-Means Clustering Playground

centroids + inertia

Why this matters

Clustering tries to discover structure without class labels. K-means is fast and useful, but its result depends on k, geometry, scale, and initialization. “A cluster” is a mathematical grouping—not automatically a meaningful real-world category.

Math underneath

assign: c(i)=arg minⱼ ||xᵢ−μⱼ||²
update: μⱼ = mean(points assigned to j)
inertia = Σᵢ ||xᵢ−μc(i)||²

Lab 7 · AI literacy

AI or Human? Calibration Challenge

uncertainty + evidence

Why this matters

People often overestimate their ability to identify AI-written text from style alone. This exercise focuses on calibration: are you appropriately confident when the evidence is weak?

The goal is not to become an “AI detector.” It is to learn why authorship claims require stronger evidence than a writing-style guess.

Calibration idea

Brier score = (confidence probability − outcome)²

Lower average score is better. Confident wrong answers are penalized more than cautious wrong answers.

Lab 8 · Generative AI

LLM Sampling Explorer

temperature + top-k + top-p

Why this matters

A language model produces scores (logits) for possible next tokens. Sampling controls transform those scores into a restricted probability distribution. Different settings can make outputs more deterministic or more diverse.

This is a transparent simulator, not a live LLM, so you can see every probability.

Math underneath

P(i)=exp(zᵢ/T) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ/T)
top-k: retain k highest-probability tokens
top-p: retain smallest set whose cumulative P ≥ p
